Murilo'sChronicles

My résumé became a tabletop RPG campaign: a miniature 3D board where every stop along the trail is a chapter of my career.

Why I built it

Nobody remembers a PDF résumé. I wanted one that makes you want to explore, and that shows off 3D in the browser, physics, sound and performance along the way.

The experience I wanted to create

The feeling of opening a board game on a tavern table: everything can be touched, played with and broken, and my career unfolds as you play.

IThe original idea

A career turned campaign

A tavern table with a board on top. The hero moves from stop to stop, the camera flies over, and each chapter opens on a scroll sealed with the companies' wax seals.

Around the board, the table fills in the rest: the cards are my projects, the character sheet is my summary and the witch's potions are my tech stack, each in its brand color.

IIHow it grew

From scenery to game

  1. Early days

    The miniature world

    Water, a boat, a lighthouse, the sun village, the castle, the parchment character sheet and dice with real physics that roll downhill and fall into the river.

  2. Soon after

    Running well on any PC

    Four graphics presets and automatic quality scaling, so it looks great on an everyday laptop.

  3. The table responds

    Almost everything is clickable

    Forging to the hammer's rhythm, fishing off the pier, archery, a mine with dynamite, the witch's cauldron and a lantern festival.

  4. It became a game

    Levels and challenges

    23 Elden Ring-style challenges, one of them secret. Every discovery earns XP, all the way to max level.

  5. For everyone

    Mobile and first person

    First-person exploration, an on-screen joystick on mobile and a warning when the browser runs on the wrong graphics card.

IIITechnical evolution

What's under the table

  1. 01

    Hand-rolled physics

    The dice run on custom physics: gravity, bounce, friction, and collisions with each other, the board's terrain and the buildings. When they stop, they settle on the face most aligned with the ground and report the number.

  2. 02

    From thousands to hundreds of draw calls

    The wide view hit 1,300 draw calls and 6 million triangles. I grouped copies of the same model into per-region instances and added LOD: from afar, trees swap to simplified versions and small details stop being drawn.

  3. 03

    Cheaper reflections and shadows

    The water reflection was redrawing the entire scene and eating a quarter of the CPU. Now it renders before the main pass, and only what can actually show up in the reflection.

  4. 04

    Quality that adapts

    Four graphics presets and automatic FPS-based scaling, tested with a CPU throttled to a quarter of its speed, plus a warning when the browser runs on integrated graphics.

  5. 05

    Terrain from hexagons

    The map is drawn on an easy-to-edit hex grid and turned into continuous terrain: cell heights become smooth hills and plateaus, and the river and road become curves.

  6. 06

    Lag-free sound

    Key sounds are decoded ahead of time, with a fallback when the browser blocks Web Audio, and heavy audio only loads after the intro.
